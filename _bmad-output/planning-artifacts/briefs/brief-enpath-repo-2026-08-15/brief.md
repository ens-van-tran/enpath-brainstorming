# Product Brief: En-Path

## Executive Snapshot

En-Path is an employee-centered capability-development product for growing companies. It gives employees transparent competency expectations, visible career gaps, employee-owned Development Plans, evidence tracking, and on-demand competency reassessment. Managers remain the official competency validators, while HR and Learning & Development govern the framework and connect individual development with company capability priorities.

The product supports continuous career development between formal review cycles. It does not replace performance review, calibration, compensation, promotion approval, or workforce-planning systems.

The rebuilt prototype will demonstrate this employee-led development loop to prospective customers and internal stakeholders. It succeeds when target buyers connect the workflow to a real organizational problem and want to continue the conversation or explore a pilot. Positive feedback about the interface alone is not validation.

## Problem And Operating Shift

Growing companies often manage competencies through spreadsheets, periodic assessments, and disconnected personal notes. HR distributes templates to managers, formal assessment happens only once or twice per year, and development actions lose visibility between cycles. Managers must reconstruct employee capability from incomplete memory, while employees cannot clearly see how the company evaluates them, what their role requires, how they can progress, or when new evidence can be recognized.

En-Path shifts this model from manager-led chasing and periodic form completion to an employee-initiated, evidence-backed development loop:

1. HR governs roles, levels, competency expectations, career paths, and company capability priorities.
2. A manager creates a baseline assessment; each employee completes a self-assessment before the manager records the official competency ratings.
3. The employee sees current-role gaps, explores reachable Target Positions, and compares the official competency profile with target-position expectations.
4. The employee creates and manages development actions, records progress, and attaches evidence.
5. When new evidence demonstrates improvement, the employee requests reassessment of a specific competency instead of waiting for the next formal cycle.

## Target Customer And Buying Motion

The initial customer is a growing company with roughly 30 or more employees, a people-development budget, and a need to build internal capability for expansion into new domains or project opportunities. Its ability to win and deliver new work depends on knowing whether employees have the required capabilities and developing those capabilities fast enough to reduce reliance on repeated hiring or restructuring.

The buying group includes:

- **Executive sponsor or economic buyer:** a CEO or senior leader funding organizational capability development.
- **Functional champion and implementation owner:** HR or Learning & Development, responsible for framework governance and rollout.
- **Adoption gate:** managers, whose participation is required for coaching, evidence review, and official competency validation.
- **Primary beneficiary and active user:** employees, whose sustained participation produces the development data and sustains the value loop.

The purchase trigger appears when leadership recognizes that daily delivery is consuming employee attention while capability growth remains passive and untracked. Expected outcomes include developing and retaining employees over the long term, improving capability visibility, and providing better competency context for adjacent processes such as performance review. These outcomes remain hypotheses to validate.

## Core MVP Experience

### Employee-Led Development

An employee can view the official competency profile, a separate self-assessment, transparent criteria, current-role expectations, and the resulting gaps. Missing assessment data remains unknown rather than being treated as zero.

HR configures the Career Path as connected `Role + Level` positions and defines which positions are reachable from each node. An employee can select a reachable Target Position to compare the official competency profile with the position's expectations. Target selection requires no proposal or manager agreement and creates no promotion promise or company commitment.

The employee may use those gaps to create a Development Plan with multiple action items. The plan remains usable without manager approval. Manager review is optional and employee-initiated; `Reviewed` means the manager examined the plan and may have commented, not that the company committed budget, mentoring, project allocation, or time. Managers can attach curated learning resources to competencies; AI-generated recommendations are deferred.

Employees manage action status and attach text, files, or links as evidence. Completed actions and evidence can support a later competency reassessment request.

### Continuous Assessment And Trust

The baseline assessment keeps the employee's self-assessment and the manager assessment separate for comparison. The manager may view the submitted self-assessment before completing the manager assessment, but only the manager assessment produces the official competency ratings.

For reassessment, the employee submits evidence for a specific competency. If the line manager lacks direct context, the manager may ask a project manager, team lead, or close collaborator for confidential feedback or evidence. Contextual reviewers cannot change ratings; the line manager retains final accountability.

The employee does not see reviewer identities or raw feedback, but the final result cannot be a black box. The manager must communicate the rating, applicable criteria, recognized evidence, decision rationale, and the next action or evidence needed. The result becomes official without employee acknowledgment. A dissatisfied employee may later submit a new request with materially new evidence or a specific criterion question.

### Organization Capability Payoff

The MVP includes a lightweight, read-only Organization Capability Overview for executives and HR. HR or leadership can define a small set of Company Capability Priorities by selecting a competency, target level, number of employees required at that level, and target date.

The overview compares those targets with official competency ratings and shows how many employees have related active Development Plans. Official ratings represent current capability; Development Plans represent development coverage and are not counted as achieved capability. This view demonstrates buyer value without expanding into project staffing, team composition, vacancy planning, or full workforce planning.

## Confirmed MVP Scope

| Role | Prototype scope |
| --- | --- |
| HR and Learning & Development | Organization structure, roles and levels, competency rubrics, Career Path configuration, Company Capability Priorities, and the Organization Capability Overview. |
| Line manager | Team-specific competencies and resources, baseline assessment, official ratings, optional Development Plan review, contextual-review requests, and final reassessment decisions. |
| Employee | Competency profile and gaps, self-assessment, Career Path and Target Position comparison, Development Plan and evidence management, optional plan-review request, and competency reassessment. |
| Contextual reviewer | A scoped, confidential response containing feedback or evidence, with no rating authority or access to unrelated employee data. |

The detailed role-based scope and workflow guardrails are preserved in the accompanying Addendum.

## Explicitly Outside MVP

- Full performance review, calibration, compensation, promotion approval, and succession decisions.
- Project staffing, team formation, vacancy planning, and opportunity matching.
- Workflows requiring a Target Position proposal or manager agreement.
- AI-generated development actions or learning-resource recommendations.
- LMS integrations, broad enterprise integrations, advanced import or export, and production-grade administration.
- Gamification, badges, and separate intermediate-recognition states.
- Blind manager scoring, employee acknowledgment gates, and contextual-reviewer scoring authority.

## Differentiation And Validation

Existing products already cover significant parts of competency management, career paths, development planning, performance workflows, and talent intelligence. En-Path cannot differentiate simply by putting competency information in one place.

The working wedge is the evidence-linked continuous loop in which an employee completes development work, attaches contextual proof, requests reassessment of one competency when the evidence becomes available, and receives the manager's official decision without waiting six to twelve months. This is a differentiation hypothesis, not a proven market gap.

Strong signals from prototype validation include a stakeholder naming a real use case or pilot population, introducing the relevant implementation owner, offering access to users or data, or asking what would be required to trial the product.

## Open Questions

- Which industry or project-delivery model should be the first target segment?
- Who owns the initial budget, who champions rollout, and whose approval can block adoption?
- Which business outcome should a pilot measure, and how will it be measured?
- Is evidence-linked, employee-triggered, on-demand competency reassessment valuable and differentiated enough to be the leading wedge?
