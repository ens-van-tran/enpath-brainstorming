- (event) Created a new En-Path Product Brief to align MVP scope before rebuilding the prototype.
- (decision) En-Path is intended as one shared competency system connecting HR governance, manager assessment, and employee development visibility.
- (decision) Employees should self-assess, track development actions and evidence, and request targeted reassessment; managers remain responsible for official rating changes.
- (assumption) Employee-initiated updates and reassessment requests will reduce manager follow-up workload; this still requires validation.
- (assumption) The market lacks an adequate end-to-end solution; a landscape check is required before using this as a product claim.
- (event) Learning-resource recommendations are desired by companies and employees but are not yet confirmed as MVP scope.
- (event) Captured current-state discovery for HR, line managers, and employees, including spreadsheet fragmentation and six-to-twelve-month review gaps.
- (override) Replaced the broad no-existing-tool claim after a landscape check found multiple partial and near-full competitors; differentiation now centers on evidence-linked employee-triggered reassessment.
- (assumption) The evidence-linked on-demand reassessment loop is a valuable market wedge; this requires customer validation.
- (decision) En-Path centers on proactive employee career development and will not replace the full performance-review process.
- (decision) The employee is the primary beneficiary and active driver, the line manager is coach and competency validator, and HR is the governance enabler.
- (decision) The next prototype is for prospective-customer and internal demonstrations to persuade stakeholders and collect feedback rather than prove operational completeness.
- (event) The user selected the coaching path so product decisions will be challenged one at a time before MVP scope is drafted.
- (decision) The target buying group includes CEOs and senior leaders, HR and Learning and Development leaders, and managers responsible for company and people growth.
- (assumption) Senior leadership is the likely executive sponsor, HR or Learning and Development the likely rollout champion, and managers the adoption gate; exact ownership remains unconfirmed.
- (decision) The initial customer profile is a growing organization with roughly 30 or more employees, a people-development budget, and a need to build internal skills for expansion.
- (decision) Senior managers and the CEO initiate solution discovery when capability gaps constrain growth, project readiness, workforce adaptation, or retention.
- (assumption) Centralized competency data can improve project qualification and team formation; this introduces a workforce-planning use case that is not yet confirmed for MVP.
- (assumption) En-Path can reduce replacement hiring and improve retention by enabling visible, continuous employee development; measurable evidence is not yet defined.
- (decision) Performance review may consume En-Path competency context as a downstream benefit but remains outside the product center.
- (decision) The leading value proposition is to align proactive individual capability development with both personal career direction and organizational priorities.
- (decision) Organization-level skill visibility and project readiness are downstream benefits rather than the leading product story.
- (decision) The experience must remain lightweight and avoid becoming another administratively heavy HR process.
- (assumption) Aligned continuous development may reduce reactive replacement hiring and restructuring, but En-Path cannot guarantee either will be eliminated.
- (assumption) En-Path can motivate sustained employee participation; the concrete motivation mechanisms still need to be defined and validated.
- (decision) Employee engagement will be built on transparent criteria, visible current and target gaps, shared action plans, evidence-backed progress, and timely company recognition rather than gamification.
- (decision) The experience must distinguish employee self-assessment, official company-recognized competency, current-role expectations, and target-role expectations.
- (decision) Every displayed gap should have understandable criteria and an achievable next action so the product supports agency rather than merely exposing a deficit.
- (assumption) Continuous company recognition will reinforce employee participation; the exact recognition event and frequency remain undefined.
- (decision) The MVP uses an Agreed Development Target jointly owned by the employee and manager; it drives official gap analysis and the development plan.
- (decision) A personal aspiration that has not been agreed with the manager will not be maintained as a separate formal target in MVP.
- (decision) The employee owns action execution and evidence while the manager ensures the target and plan are realistic and aligned with organizational direction.
- (decision) For a competency reassessment request, the Line Manager may assign contextual reviewers such as a Project Manager, Team Lead, or closely involved employee when direct context is insufficient.
- (decision) Contextual reviewers provide feedback and evidence only; they cannot change the official competency rating.
- (decision) The Line Manager retains final accountability and makes the official competency decision after reviewing the complete evidence record.
- (assumption) Contextual review can improve assessment quality without creating a full peer-review or multi-rater scoring workflow.
- (decision) Employees cannot see contextual reviewer identity or raw feedback; these remain available to the Line Manager and internal decision record.
- (decision) Employee acknowledgement is not required for an official competency result and must not be interpreted as agreement.
- (decision) A dissatisfied employee may submit a new request to the Line Manager with questions and additional evidence instead of acknowledging the prior result.
- (assumption) A sufficiently clear Line Manager explanation can preserve trust even when contextual reviewer input remains confidential.
- (decision) Every reassessment result must include the final rating, applicable criteria or rubric, recognized evidence, decision rationale, and next action or evidence needed.
- (decision) The five-field explanation is required even when the rating remains unchanged; a bare score or generic rejection is insufficient.
- (decision) Only one reassessment request may be active for a competency at a time.
- (decision) A repeated request must add materially new evidence or a specific question about the criteria or prior decision; duplicates without new information are not reopened.
- (decision) Reassessment has no fixed waiting period when new information exists, and the current official rating remains effective until a new decision is completed.
- (decision) The MVP does not add a separate recognition status or reward for accepted evidence when the official competency rating remains unchanged.
- (decision) Evidence recognition and the reason for no rating change remain visible inside the mandatory reassessment explanation; richer intermediate recognition is deferred.
- (override) Refined plan ownership: the Agreed Development Target is jointly owned, but the Development Plan and action items are employee-authored and do not require manager approval.
- (decision) Manager review of a Development Plan is optional and initiated by the employee; without a request the employee continues using the plan normally.
- (decision) Employees manage multiple action items, mark them Done, and attach text, file, or link evidence that can later support reassessment.
- (decision) MVP learning resources are curated by managers and attached to competencies for employee recommendations.
- (decision) AI-generated learning-resource and action recommendations are deferred beyond the initial prototype.
- (override) Replaced manager plan approval with Reviewed because Development Plans remain employee-owned and usable without a gate.
- (decision) Reviewed confirms manager attention and may include comments or suggestions, but does not create a company commitment for budget, project allocation, mentoring, or time.
- (decision) The prototype succeeds when target buyers see enough value in the employee-led continuous-development loop to continue discussion or explore a pilot.
- (decision) Positive UI feedback alone is not product validation; stronger signals include a real use case, proposed pilot group, stakeholder introduction, or request for trial requirements.
- (decision) HR configures a company career tree of role-and-level nodes and available next positions; employees see their current node and reachable company-supported paths.
- (decision) Career-path visibility supports target agreement but does not guarantee promotion or role availability.
- (decision) After team competencies are configured, the Manager creates an assessment and assigns Employees to complete self-assessment first.
- (decision) Manager assessment produces the official company-recognized competency result while Employee self-assessment remains a separate comparison series.
- (decision) The competency assessment establishes a development baseline and is not a full performance-review cycle.
- (assumption) Manager viewing Employee self-assessment before rating may create anchoring bias; visibility timing remains unresolved.
- (decision) The Manager may view the submitted Employee self-assessment before recording their own rating; blind or independent scoring is unnecessary because the Manager rating is the final company-recognized result.
- (override) Closed the self-assessment visibility question: anchoring controls are not an MVP concern; the Manager may see the submitted self-assessment because their rating remains final.
- (decision) The MVP prototype includes a lightweight read-only Organization Capability Overview for executives and HR, limited to aggregate competency gaps and development activity rather than project staffing or workforce planning.
- (decision) HR or leadership can define a small set of Company Capability Priorities in MVP using competency, target level, required employee count, and target date.
- (decision) Organization strategic gaps use official Manager-recognized ratings as current capability, while related active Development Plans are shown separately as development coverage and not counted as achieved capability.
- (override) Simplified the Agreed Development Target to one Role and Level position selected from the Career Path; its main purpose is to show the gap from the employee's current official profile to the target-position expectations, without target types, manual competency selection, or a target date.
- (override) Removed the Agreed Development Target and proposal flow. A Target Position is an Employee-selected Role and Level used only to view competency gaps; it requires no Manager agreement, creates no company commitment, and may optionally inform the separately employee-owned Development Plan.
- (event) Drafted a candidate role-based MVP prototype scope and explicit out-of-scope list in the addendum for coaching review; it is provisional until the user confirms the scope.
- (decision) The user confirmed the role-based MVP prototype scope and explicit out-of-scope list as the boundary for the next prototype rebuild.
- (event) Finalized the Product Brief and Addendum after scope confirmation, memlog audit, structural editing, and prose editing. The brief holds the strategic direction and confirmed MVP boundary; the addendum holds detailed scope, guardrails, discovery, landscape context, and validation hypotheses; superseded decisions and workflow process notes remain only in the append-only memlog.
